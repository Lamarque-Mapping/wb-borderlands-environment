Urban settlement footprints and population · NASA SEDAC GUPPD v1CIESIN, Columbia University and JRC, European Commission (2024), Global Urban Polygons and Points Dataset, v1. Dataset and documentation.CC BY 4.0. Attribute CIESIN / NASA SEDAC and JRC.Representative points and whole urban footprints based on circa-2015 GHS-SMOD R2019 definitions. The displayed population uses GHSPOP_R23_2020, a GHSL R2023 modelled estimate inside those fixed footprints. These can combine neighbouring settlements and are not current municipal boundaries or town census totals. The map cohort is source representative points inside each cluster, paired with their source polygons by country and SMOD ID; polygons that merely intersect a cluster without their point inside it are excluded from this view. Source fields and full geometries are preserved. Points and polygons are not counted twice, overlapping cluster selections are not summed, and missing names are not guessed. The source also includes later projections, which are not displayed as observations. GUPPD does not replace the OSM places used in the existing 10 km junction metric. Relative deprivation · NASA SEDAC GRDI v1CIESIN, Columbia University (2022), Global Gridded Relative Deprivation Index, v1; inputs span 2010–2020. Dataset and documentation.CC BY 4.0. Attribute CIESIN / NASA SEDAC.Approximately 1 km index from 0 to 100, with higher values indicating more deprivation. It is not the percentage of people in poverty. Inputs include child dependency, infant mortality, subnational human development, built-up area and night-time light levels/change; some vary only at broad administrative scales. Source building-footprint coverage leaves substantial gaps, displayed as no data, never zero or evidence of no population. The analysis shows coverage and approximate cell-area-weighted means/distributions among valid pixels, without population weighting. Inputs overlap with existing lights and settlement layers, so the indicators are not independent evidence. Display bands are common across maps and are chosen for visual comparison, not official poverty categories. Raster values and source-grid alignment are retained; the overview image uses nearest-neighbour reprojection, and click values use the original grid. No admin-unit join is inferred. Historical humid heat · NASA SEDAC GEHETuholske, Peterson, Funk and Caylor (2023), Annual Global High-Resolution Extreme Heat Estimates, 1983–2016. Dataset and documentation.CC BY 4.0. Attribute the authors / NASA SEDAC.Approximately 5 km annual counts of days above daily maximum wet-bulb globe temperature (WBGT) thresholds of 28, 30 and 32°C. WBGT incorporates humidity; it is not ordinary air temperature. Maps show arithmetic means of all 34 labelled years, requiring valid observations in every year. Annual analysis uses the same valid locations, weighted by approximate grid-cell area, without population weighting. Zero days and missing values remain distinct. The record ends in 2016 and does not describe present heat, individual exposure, traffic, working hours or health outcomes. Original annual rasters and significance-filtered trends were retained in the QGIS preparation; the website displays the annual counts and their averages, not trend significance as an absence-of-change test.
